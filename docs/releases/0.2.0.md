Hokum is now a control center: notifications, Sound and Bluetooth in one app.

- Sound (PipeWire): output and input volume and devices, the apps playing
  with their own volume, Bluetooth codecs, profiles, routing and the clock
  (#16, #17).
- Bluetooth (BlueZ): power, connected and paired devices with their battery,
  pairing with the code shown inline, and adapters (#16, #17).
- One tray icon opens a quick panel: output volume and device, the
  microphone while something records, Bluetooth devices and Do not disturb.
  The settings window's sidebar is Notifications, Sound and Bluetooth (#16).
- Both modules are on by default. Turn them off with
  `modules { sound #false; bluetooth #false }` in `config.kdl`; a module that
  can't start shows one line in its section and never stops notifications.
- A volume card shows when the output volume changes (`osd #false` in
  `modules` turns it off), and cards say when a Bluetooth device connects or
  its battery runs low (`bluetooth-notifications #false`) (#17).
- New commands: `hokum sound` and `hokum bt` open the window on that section.
- The settings window follows the desktop's light or dark preference. Thanks
  @salemsayed, also for the Rune fix (#20, #21).
- On GNOME, Plasma or a quickshell setup that already owns notifications,
  Hokum says so plainly instead of restarting in a loop, and it exits on
  compositors without layer-shell. Thanks @AMluisXVI and @Redouane64 (#10, #24).
- Card shadows are no longer cut off on the side away from the screen edge.
  Thanks @uncomplexity (#3, #9).

Packaging: Hokum now depends on libpipewire and recommends wireplumber and
bluez.
